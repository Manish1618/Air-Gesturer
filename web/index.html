How it reads your hand

The tracker finds 21 points on your hand in every frame: the wrist, the knuckles and each joint of every finger.

A finger counts as raised when its tip is clearly farther from the wrist than its middle joint. The pattern of raised fingers gives the pose: one finger is a point, two is a peace sign, all four is an open palm, none is a fist.

Movement is measured in palm widths rather than pixels, so the same gesture works whether your hand is close to the camera or across the room.

Why the delay?

A new pose has to hold for two frames before it counts. That stops a hand passing through a pose on its way to another from triggering anything.

How it works

  1. Capture. The browser reads your webcam and mirrors it like a selfie.
  2. Detect. Google's MediaPipe hand model finds 21 landmarks per frame.
  3. Scale. Motion is divided by the size of your palm, so distance doesn't matter.
  4. Classify. Which fingers are raised decides the pose.
  5. Settle. A new pose must hold for two frames before it counts.
  6. Track. Small state machines turn motion into scrolls, swipes, steps and holds.
  7. Act. The page scrolls, turns, changes volume, dismisses or pauses.

It started on the desktop

Air Gesturer is a Python application first. The desktop version uses OpenCV, MediaPipe and Tkinter to control the whole computer: it scrolls any window, switches workspaces, changes the system volume and closes windows.

This page runs the same gesture logic, translated from Python to JavaScript and checked against the original on 3,000 recorded hand positions.
